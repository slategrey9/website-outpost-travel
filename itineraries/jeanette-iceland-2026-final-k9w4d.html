Day 1
Wed 7 October
Arrival, Blue Lagoon, then Reykjavík

Welcome to Iceland. From the airport, you'll travel to the Blue Lagoon, Iceland's most iconic geothermal soak, perfect for easing the long-haul flight out of your shoulders. After your float, you'll continue into Reykjavík and to Sand Hotel. The rest of the afternoon is yours to decompress, settle in, and start to feel the different rhythm of the place. You land at Keflavík at 10:00am on BA800. See Your Flights above for suggested shuttle timings.

Sand Hotel will be your home base in Reykjavík. This boutique hotel is a sweet confection by the pâtissiers behind beloved Icelandic bakery Sandholt, which sits right next door. The hotel is centrally located on Reykjavík's main strip, Laugavegur; its neighbours are edgy clothing shops for wannabe Björks, an assortment of global restaurants, and cute cafés. All of the city's main sights are within easy walking distance.

Each morning, tuck into a Continental breakfast buffet at Sandbar on the ground floor. The morning feast includes house-made granola, rich coffees, and freshly baked breads and pastries from next-door neighbour Sandholt Bakery.

Day 2
Thu 8 October
The Golden Circle & First Night at Midgard

Today marks the start of your Midgard Adventure. Your guide collects you from the Sand Hotel lobby at 9:00am, bags and all, and you'll spend the day visiting Iceland's iconic Golden Circle: Þingvellir National Park, where the tectonic plates quietly pull apart, the Geysir geothermal area where Strokkur erupts every few minutes, and Gullfoss, dropping in two thundering tiers into the canyon below. Lunch is at Friðheimar Tomato Farm, a working greenhouse where they serve a really good tomato soup alongside fresh-baked bread. There is a geothermal bakery stop too, where rye bread is baked in the hot ground and dug out fresh. By evening you'll arrive at Midgard Base Camp in the heart of South Iceland, home for the next three nights. Dinner is waiting when you are, and afterwards the rooftop hot tub is yours whenever you'd like it.

Day 3
Fri 9 October
Katla Ice Caves & the South Coast

This morning the Midgard team takes you to Katla, Iceland's most powerful subglacial volcano, for an ice cave expedition beneath the glacier. The walls of the cave shift from black volcanic ash to translucent blue ice, and your guide will explain how the whole thing changes season to season. In the afternoon, you'll head to Reynisfjara where the Atlantic crashes against the basalt columns and the famous sea stacks rise from the black sand, with further stops along the south coast as time allows. Back at Midgard for dinner, and another evening under the dancing skies.

Day 4
Sat 10 October
Super Jeep Into Þórsmörk

A real favourite. Modified 4×4 Super Jeeps ford glacial rivers to reach Þórsmörk Mountain Reserve, a highland nature area so remote it can only be accessed with specialist vehicles. You'll spend the whole day in the reserve: wide river crossings, volcanic ridgelines, views across to Eyjafjallajökull volcano (the one that famously grounded European air travel in 2010), and the kind of quiet that only exists where there are no roads. Midgard also include a visit to a local Icelandic home along the way, which is always a highlight. Back at base for a traditional lamb dinner, and one more night of aurora watching to close out your time at Midgard.

Day 5
Sun 11 October
Glacier Walk & South Coast Waterfalls

The final day of your Midgard Adventure. You'll start with a guided hike on Sólheimajökull Glacier, crampons on, moving quietly between crevasses as your guide explains how the glacier has shifted over the years. Stop by the glacier lagoon afterwards, then three of Iceland's most beautiful waterfalls in one afternoon: Skógafoss thundering down from a grassy ridge, Seljalandsfoss where a path takes you right behind the cascade, and the hidden Gljúfrabúi tucked into a narrow canyon that most visitors walk straight past. In the late afternoon, you'll say your goodbyes to the Midgard team, who drop you at Aurora Igloo South, a glass-ceiling igloo with private bathroom, designed for Northern Lights viewing from the comfort of your bed. Check-in is self-service from 4:00pm: you're in Igloo 14, and the key is in the box by your door (code 7417). There's no restaurant on site, so pick up dinner and something for the morning before you arrive, or order in from Árhús in Hella, which delivers. Your igloo has an empty fridge and a Nespresso machine.

Day 6
Mon 12 October
Private "Not in Your Guidebook" Adventure

Wake up under the glass ceiling for one more look at the sky, and then step into what might be the most personal day of the whole trip. The Midgard team calls this their "Not in Your Guidebook" adventure, and they mean it literally: most of the places you'll visit aren't mentioned in any guidebook. These are the places the Midgard guides go on their own days off, where you're unlikely to see anyone other than a local traveller or a farmer. It's a truly local experience that only Midgard offers.

The area is in and around Fljótshlíð, the "hill by the river", a beautiful and peaceful stretch of countryside where many of the Icelandic Sagas took place. Depending on what the day calls for, your guide might take you to the small canyons and favourite playgrounds underneath Mt. Þórólfsfell, the 120-metre summit of Stóri Dímon for the view (or a walk around its base for hidden corners), rivers and hidden waterfalls around Mt. Þríhyrningur, and one of Iceland's few small forests where a farmer lived to 100 and an old well still stands. Taking a sip from that well is Midgard's way of staying young, and they say it could become yours too. Lunch is with your guide along the way. In the evening you'll be dropped at Sand Hotel for two nights in Reykjavík.

Reykjavík at Leisure

A full free day in the capital, which means it's whatever you want it to be. A few suggestions: Hallgrímskirkja Church, whose architecture echoes the basalt columns you'll have seen at Reynisfjara. Harpa Concert Hall on the harbour, all geometric glass and shifting colour. A walk around the Old Harbour, the historic working edge of the city, still home to fishing boats and whale-watching operators. And the Reykjavík Maritime Museum, which sits right on the harbour and tells the story of Iceland's relationship with the sea. If you'd like us to book a restaurant for you for the evening, just say the word.

Day 8
Wed 14 October
West to Húsafell

A leisurely morning in Reykjavík and a last walk through the city. Your driver meets you in the Sand Hotel lobby at 3:30pm for the private transfer west to Hotel Húsafell, about two hours through the lava fields, arriving as the light softens. Hraunfossar and Barnafoss are five minutes from the hotel, a long line of springs emerging directly from the lava field and cascading into the river below. Settle in, walk out into the surrounding nature, or simply sit by the fire and watch the light shift.

Tucked away from the city with very little light pollution, the hotel sits in one of the best spots in West Iceland for Northern Lights, and you'll have a good chance of seeing them from the comfort of your room or the lounge. If the aurora appears after you've turned in for the night, the hotel offers complimentary wake-up calls so you don't miss it.

Day 9
Thu 15 October
Slow Days, Hot Pools

This is the day we've been building up to. No schedule, no agenda, nowhere you need to be. The surrounding lava fields have hiking trails right from the hotel, perfect for a morning walk out toward the Langjökull glacier views. The one fixed point is the Húsafell Canyon Baths, which we've booked for the two of you: a set of soaking pools built into the canyon itself using locally sourced stone, reached by a short drive from the hotel and an easy guided walk down into the gorge, with Langifoss waterfall on the way. The outing runs about an hour and three quarters, with transport, guide and towels included. Bring swimwear, hiking boots and warm waterproof layers. Before or after, the hotel's own geothermal pool is right on the grounds. Settle in for a long evening by the fire and one last Húsafell dinner.

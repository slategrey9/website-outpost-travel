Day 1

Xi'an · Friday, 6 November 2026

Into the Old Capital

Arrival, the Terracotta Warriors & the Muslim Quarter · Dinner included

Though Beijing has taken the mantle as China's modern capital, its history and prestige are nothing next to the old capital of Xi'an, where emperors ruled for thousands of years. Home of the Terracotta Warriors, the eastern end of the Silk Road, and a history that stretches longer than the Great Wall, Xi'an epitomises traditional Chinese culture.

Small hours
Your overnight Scoot flight lands at Xi'an Xianyang International at 12:25am. Your driver and guide meet you in the arrivals hall and your private vehicle is on standby for the transfer to your hotel, around an hour, and a well-earned sleep.
Morning
A lie-in and a slow start to recover from the late arrival, with the morning yours to ease into the trip.
Late morning
The Terracotta Warriors. Set off for the great archaeological wonder less than an hour east of the city. In 1974, while digging a well in the countryside outside Xi'an, a group of farmers struck a chamber containing some 8,000 soldiers belonging to China's fearsome first emperor, a terracotta army buried to guard his tomb for eternity. You'll spend around two hours coming face to face with row upon row of these ancient guardians, an experience no trip to China is complete without.
Afternoon
Xi'an's Ancient City Wall. Rising to an imperious 12 metres, the oldest, largest and best-preserved city wall in China still rings the downtown. The best way to experience it is to walk along the top, taking in the commanding views of the old city within and the modern metropolis beyond.
Evening
A food tour through the Muslim Quarter. Islam flowed along the Silk Road into China some 1,300 years ago, and the Muslim Quarter its followers settled is still one of the most bustling corners of the city. A tuk-tuk whisks you through the quieter back lanes beyond the famous Muslim Street, from local hot spots to hidden gems, homemade noodles to sidewalk barbecue. Over 3.5 hours you'll sample 10 different dishes, washed down with a craft beer at a local brewery.

Day 2

Xi'an → Beijing · Saturday, 7 November 2026

High-Speed North

First-class rail north, the hutongs & a cooking class · Dinner included

Xi'an has the long history and Beijing has a little of everything. China's capital since the Yuan dynasty (1279–1368), Beijing forces its past, present and future together in the streets and crowded neighbourhoods of the city; for centuries its political power and its thriving intellectual and artistic life have shaped China and the world.

Morning
High-speed train to Beijing. Your driver and guide escort you to the station and help you check in. Settle into your first-class seats for the ride north, around four and a half hours gliding across the north China plain. On arrival, your Beijing guide meets you in the hall with your private vehicle on standby.
Xi'an → Beijing · high-speed rail · ~4.5 hrs
Afternoon
Into the hutongs: home visits and cricket prowess. The hutongs, the crisscrossing maze of courtyards and alleyways, are the heart of old Beijing; locals will tell you anyone who lives further out isn't really a Beijinger. You'll pass elders at chess and mahjong and shopkeepers selling jianbing and baozi, and stop by the homes of some of the city's more traditional residents, including the courtyard of a man who has won fame through the legendary prowess of his fighting crickets. The crickets are treated with extraordinary care, their own special brushes, ginger-infused water in carved bowls, even hand-whittled coffins for the very end; a corner of Beijing culture only the most native of locals know.
Evening
A private cooking class. If family is the nucleus of Chinese culture, food is what binds it together. Expert chefs take you to source ingredients at a bustling local market, then show you how to turn them into mouthwatering seasonal dishes back at their private school, hidden in the courtyard of a traditional hutong, before you sit down to eat together.
Private transfers both ends · first-class train between cities

Day 3

Beijing · Sunday, 8 November 2026

The Imperial City

Tiananmen Square, the Forbidden City & the Summer Palace

Morning
Tiananmen Square and the Forbidden City. Start in Tiananmen Square, the vast ceremonial heart of the city, then pass through the Meridian Gate into the Forbidden City, the massive imperial palace at the geographic centre of Beijing. Its gates were shut to all but the royal household and their entourage of eunuchs and concubines for 600 years, until in 1924 a warlord gave the last emperor just three hours to leave.
Afternoon
The Summer Palace. An afternoon of royal splendour half an hour from the city, its pathways and pavilions built for the Empress Dowager Cixi, a former concubine who became the most powerful woman in China. Unwind from her tales of intrigue with a leisurely boat ride across Kunming Lake, the garden's centrepiece, dug by hand at Cixi's behest.
Evening
At leisure, a quieter evening to rest, or your guide can point you to dinner nearby.
The Forbidden City is on a fixed timed ticket and, once confirmed, this booking cannot be changed.

Day 4

Beijing · Monday, 9 November 2026

The Great Wall at Mutianyu

The Great Wall at Mutianyu, then an afternoon at leisure

Morning / early afternoon
The Great Wall at Mutianyu. Drive north to one of the best-preserved and most scenic stretches of the Ming wall, immense brickwork snaking through rugged terrain where imperial guards once watched for invaders from remote outposts. A gondola carries you up, and from there you can walk a full two-hour stretch of the ramparts, or take it at an easier pace, with the autumn hills falling away on either side.

Back down by gondola, or by toboggan for anyone who fancies it, usually a hit with the kids.
Beijing → Mutianyu · ~2 hrs each way
Late afternoon
Return to Beijing, with the rest of the day free to rest or shop; the Wangfujing and Qianmen streets are good for both.
Driving today Beijing → Mutianyu return · ~2 hrs each way

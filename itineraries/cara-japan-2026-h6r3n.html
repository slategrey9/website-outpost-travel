Day 1

Tokyo · Friday, 16 October 2026

Arrival in Tokyo

One of the world’s most cutting-edge capitals, Tokyo is a city of contrasts. Famous for its sprawling neon-lit landscape it is also home to expansive parkland, peaceful shrines and temples, and lovingly tended gardens. On the surface, Tokyo is a mix of digital trends and conspicuous consumption, but dig deeper and you will find a city rooted deeply in the traditional culture. Shinto shrines and Buddhist temples stand close to skyscrapers as a reminder of a more contemplative time. At the heart of the hyperactive centre lies the serene Imperial Palace, the home of the ruling emperor that provides a tangible link to the city’s historical past. Behind the shopping, entertainment, and commercial emporia you will find quaint wooden houses, private gardens with meticulously clipped bonsai trees and the calm of the Hamarikyu Gardens.

Day 2

Tokyo · Saturday, 17 October 2026

Full Day Tokyo Highlights Tour

Take a full-day tour of this fascinating city with a local guide. First up is Asakusa, Tokyo’s old town where you can soak in the atmosphere of traditional Tokyo. Visit Sensoji, Tokyo’s oldest temple and wander down Nakamise, a shopping street that has been providing temple visitors with a variety of traditional, local snacks and tourist souvenirs for centuries.

Next, travel to Hamarikyu Garden, an Edo-period Japanese garden surrounded by the Shiodome district’s futuristic skyscrapers, a great example of how Japan is the land of contrasts. Here, you will stop for a cup of steaming matcha and Japanese sweets in a tea house on a small island in the park’s lake.

Later, enjoy a visit to Meiji Shrine, a shrine dedicated to the deified spirit of Emperor Meiji and a popular place for traditional Japanese weddings. Finally, as the sunlight fades, take a walk down the flashy Omotesando shopping street, a broad tree-lined avenue home to flagship stores of some of the world’s top fashion brands as well as some fantastic modern architecture.

Finish the day with a visit to Shibuya, one of Japan’s major shopping and entertainment districts, and home of the famous 5-way ‘scramble crossing’.

You will make your own way home from Shibuya Station at the end of the tour.

Day 3

Tokyo · Sunday, 18 October 2026

Full Day ‘The Future is Tokyo’ Tour

When the sprawling metropolis of Tokyo ran out of space, city planners inevitably turned to the sea. Odaiba is a man-made island created by massive landfills featuring many hypermodern and strange buildings, memorably described as the result of a pre-schooler’s architecture class. Marvelling at the exhibition pavilions, shopping malls, game centres, cafés, restaurants, and surrealistic constructions of Odaiba, visitors will undoubtedly feel intrigued by the island’s majestic structures that seem to hail from the future rather than the past.

Visit teamLab Planets, which, together with now-closed teamLab Borderless, is also affectionately referred to as the ‘Instagram Museum’. Inside, projection mapping cleverly melds with human interaction, creating a fantasy world of illusions shaking the very foundations of what art is.

Next, visit Small Worlds, a highly interactive museum of hand-built miniature worlds, standing in for the Museum of Emerging Science while it is closed for renovation.

Take a ride on Tokyo’s monorail back to the mainland and visit Akihabara. Also known as Electric Town, Akihabara is famous for its hundreds of electronic shops selling a mindboggling range of gadgets, gizmos, and similar devices. In recent times, however, Akihabara has become even more famous for its ‘otaku’ culture. The term otaku is used about those obsessed with cosplay, manga, anime, gaming, or other aspects of Japanese pop-culture, and the many stores of Akihabara cater to all kinds of otaku. Even the nearby Kanda Shrine has an anime twist to its story.

This tour will start and end at your accommodation.

Day 5

Tokyo → Hakone · Tuesday, 20 October 2026

Full Day Hakone Excursion from Tokyo

Hakone is a natural wonderland and is famous for its hot springs, outdoor pursuits and the view of the nearby Mt. Fuji. It is part of the Fuji-Hakone-Izu National Park and is less than 100km from Tokyo, thereby providing a popular weekend destination for city residents keen to escape the frenzy of the capital. Beautiful Hakone has about everything a vacationer could wish for. Besides the towering mountains, lakes and views of Mt. Fuji, it is also blessed with interesting historical sites.

Today, leave Tokyo behind as you journey to Hakone, one of the gateways to Mt. Fuji and the surrounding national park.

Travel to Hakone to explore the Hakone ‘Loop’. The loop can be done in a clockwise or anti-clockwise direction and typically includes a journey up to the Gora area to ride the Hakone Ropeway to the Owakudani volcano crater. Here, you can try the tasty black eggs boiled in water bubbling up from within the volcano itself. Also included in today’s arrangements is a cruise across Lake Ashi before checking in to your accommodation in Hakone.

On this tour you might be lucky to catch a glimpse of Mt. Fuji, but please note that she is a notoriously shy mountain and the weather needs to be perfectly clear for her to be visible. Visibility is best from November to March.

Hakone → Kyoto · Wednesday, 21 October 2026

Travel to Kyoto

This is a must-see destination in Japan. Kyoto is the nation’s former capital and was the residence of the emperor from 794 until 1868. With 2,000 religious buildings, including 1,600 Buddhist temples and 400 Shinto shrines, as well as palaces, gardens and associated architecture, it is one of the best-preserved cities in Japan and has 17 UNESCO World Heritage sites. Kyoto represents the ‘Japan of old’ and beyond the high-rise skyscrapers built as a monument to progress, the real monument to Japan’s historical and cultural past can be found in the city’s narrow alleyways where tea houses abound and kimono-clad geisha hurry from elegant function to function.

Day 7

Kyoto · Thursday, 22 October 2026

Full Day Kyoto and Arashiyama Highlights Tour

From dramatic power struggles that led to almost 200 years of civil war, to the oldest geisha district and its beautifully preserved teahouses, northern Kyoto is rich in history and spectacular architecture.

Kinkakuji Temple draws millions of visitors each year, and you will quickly understand why this jewel in the northern hills of Kyoto is one of the country’s most visited sites. A far cry from Buddhist austerity, it is covered in real gold leaf, photogenically stunning at any time of the year no matter the weather. The story of the warlord who had this pavilion built will surely capture your mind, too.

Visit Nijo Castle, a 17th century castle originally constructed for a shogun, and today a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Marvel at the castle’s architecture and unique features such as the intricate sliding doors, chirping nightingale floors and secret chambers.

Next, walk through Nishiki Market, which will mesmerise you with rows and stands selling snacks and ingredients for cooking.

Lastly, head to the Arashiyama district, a pleasant area in the western outskirts of Kyoto. Arashiyama has been a popular destination since the Heian period (794-1185), when nobles would relax in its natural settings. Take a walk through the famous bamboo groves, which are particularly attractive when a light wind makes the tall bamboo stalks sway gently back and forth. The bamboo has been used at local workshops for centuries to create various products, such as baskets, cups, boxes and mats.

This tour will start and end at your accommodation.

NotesNishiki Market is open every day, but about one quarter of the shops are closed on Wednesdays. Your visit falls on a Thursday, so this should not affect you.

Samurai Sword Dance Show and Experience

Learn about the legendary samurai warriors during this exciting tour. You will witness kenbu: a highly stylised sword dance that samurai used to perform as training and to find focus.

You can take photos with the sword masters and have a lesson about samurai etiquette using a katana or a folding fan.

The kenbu masters will be happy to answer any questions you might have.

You will make your own way to and from this experience.

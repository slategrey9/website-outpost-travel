China is a country of people and their stories: of ancient dynasties and living traditions, sacred mountains and sprawling megacities. This journey brings the whole family to Sichuan, meeting the pandas, riding up a sacred Taoist mountain and wandering the teahouses and temples of Chengdu, with local guides who share the history, culture and everyday life behind each place.

The trip begins in the mountains, with three nights at Six Senses Qing Cheng Mountain, with plenty of time to unwind at the resort. It finishes with three nights in Chengdu, Sichuan’s laid-back capital, with a free day on New Year’s Eve.

Most days have one or two main visits and are intentionally designed with your toddlers in mind, with ample downtime to unwind at the hotel. You’ll get around in a private vehicle with your own driver and guide.

Day 2 · Sunday, 27 December

Qingcheng Mountain and Jiezi Old Town

Qingcheng Mountain

Qingcheng Mountain is one of the birthplaces of Taoism. Known as “the most peaceful and secluded under heaven”, it is where Zhang Daoling founded the Way of the Celestial Masters in the 2nd century AD, and it is now a UNESCO World Heritage Site together with the nearby Dujiangyan irrigation system.

Qingcheng Mountain. Ride the cable car up the mountain, the views opening up as it falls away, then walk as much or as little of the trail as you like, exploring the temples along the way.

Jiezi Old Town. The streets of Jiezi Old Town have little changed over the centuries. As pilgrimage to Qingcheng Mountain grew, the town stayed as it was, a quaint collection of twisting streets, old teahouses, and a temple from the Jin dynasty (265–420 AD). As you stroll the streets, ducking into shops tucked away in side alleys, keep an ear out for the splashing and laughing of local children playing in the river.

Day 3 · Monday, 28 December

Red Panda Forest Park

Qingcheng Mountain

A morning with the red pandas in the forests of Dujiangyan, then the rest of the day to enjoy the resort, with the kids’ club, the farm and the indoor pool all on hand.

Red Panda Forest Park. Nestled in the lush surroundings of Dujiangyan, the Red Panda Forest Park is a specialised conservation and education centre dedicated to the protection and breeding of red pandas. Rather than a traditional zoo, the park offers visitors the chance to observe these charming animals in naturalistic forest settings and learn about their behaviour, conservation, and daily lives. It is a particularly engaging experience for families and wildlife enthusiasts, with opportunities for closer encounters under the guidance of the park’s professional staff.

Day 4 · Tuesday, 29 December

On to Chengdu: Qingyang, Wuhou and Wenshu Temples

Qingcheng Mountain → Chengdu

Back down to Chengdu after lunch, then visit three of the region’s most historically significant temples.

Drive to Chengdu. After lunch, drive from Qingcheng Mountain to Chengdu, which will take around 1.5 to 2 hours. Upon arrival, check in at the hotel.

Next stop: Chengdu. There is a popular saying: “Once you arrive in Chengdu, you never want to leave.” Spend even a few days here and it is easy to understand why. In Sichuan’s laid-back capital, life unfolds over cups of jasmine tea, lively mahjong games, bubbling hot pot, and shaded park paths. Yet Chengdu is more than slow living. It is a dynamic city of students, artists, entrepreneurs, and food lovers, where old teahouses sit alongside creative neighbourhoods and contemporary restaurants. Home to the beloved giant panda and some of China’s boldest flavours, Chengdu offers a warm, vivid introduction to Sichuan’s culture, creativity, and famously unhurried way of life.

Qingyang Temple. Qingyang Taoist Temple is the birthplace of Lao Zi, the founding father of Taoism, which promotes the principle of wuwei, “do nothing.” Hidden throughout the grounds, under the open sky, nestled amidst the leaves, are small idols to different Taoist deities. Discovering them is discovering both the temple and Taoist thought.

Wuhou Temple. Wuhou Temple is the resting place of Zhuge Liang, one of the most famous military strategists in China. The Martial Marquis of Shu during the Three Kingdoms period (220–280 AD), Zhuge Liang’s wit and tact have rendered him immortal in military tales, and statues around the temple celebrate his life and accomplishments. After your visit, make your way to the local teahouses and shops lining the nearby Jinli street.

Wenshu Monastery. Wenshu Monastery is one of China’s largest and most well-preserved buildings of its kind. Its sloping roofs, upwardly flicked at the corners, towering colonnades, and incense-perfumed halls date back over 1,000 years. Your exploration of this working historical holy site allows you to experience the tranquil atmosphere and observe the daily rituals of monks and nuns, offering a glimpse into the spiritual life of the monastery.

Day 5 · Wednesday, 30 December

Panda Base, Kung Fu Tea and the Kuanzhai Alleys

Chengdu

In the morning, get up close to the pandas that call Chengdu home. In the afternoon, immerse yourself in the city’s culture with a kung fu tea performance and a stroll down the Kuanzhai Alleys.

Chengdu Panda Base. Despite making their way into zoos around the world as diplomatic gifts from China in the 1970s, the best place to see everybody’s favourite black and white bears is in their hometown, Chengdu. In the Chengdu Panda Breeding Center, you will learn about the diet, reproduction, and conservation of these adorable creatures, and have plenty of opportunities to watch them do what they do best: eat, sleep, and roll around.

Kung Fu tea performance. China’s traditions of tea and kung fu are almost as old as the country itself. It is the marriage of the two, in the form of kung fu tea, that is much newer and wholly unique to Chengdu. Gaze in awe as the performer carries out a series of dazzling movements inspired by tai chi and kung fu, all culminating in the tea landing artfully in the cup. Then, awaken your inner tea master as you try your hand at gracefully pouring your own cup before tasting the fruits of your labour. After the performance, wander through the park for the ultimate people-watching experience, watching the locals as they dance their cares away or enjoy a roaring game of mahjong.

Kuanzhai Alleys. The Kuanzhai Alleys are a dynamic collision of traditional architecture and modern culture, where Qing Dynasty buildings play host to some of Chengdu’s best coffee shops, bars, galleries and boutiques. Rub shoulders with locals who love to roam these quirky lanes on their day off and tuck into some of the city’s traditional snacks.

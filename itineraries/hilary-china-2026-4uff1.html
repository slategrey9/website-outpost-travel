Thirteen days,
four stops

Chengdu provides a gentle start, with pandas, teahouses and temples, followed by two nights at Qingcheng Mountain. Xi’an makes a particularly atmospheric base for Christmas, with the Terracotta Warriors and the ancient City Wall. Beijing is the final stop, with the Forbidden City, the hutongs and the Great Wall at Mutianyu.

Most days have one or two main visits and are intentionally designed with your toddlers in mind, with ample downtime to unwind at the hotel. You’ll get around in a private vehicle with your own driver and guide, while the journeys from Chengdu to Xi’an (around 3 to 3.5 hours) and from Xi’an to Beijing (around 4.5 to 5 hours) are by high-speed train.

Where you’ll stay

Each of the four properties offers a refined and luxurious stay, while reflecting the local culture and heritage of its city. All four have a heated indoor pool.

Chengdu · Nights 1 to 3

Upper House Chengdu

Three nights, bed and breakfast · 18 to 21 December

Formerly The Temple House, Upper House Chengdu sits right in the centre of the city, within Taikoo Li, Chengdu’s open-air shopping and dining quarter of restored heritage lanes and international boutiques, beside the 1,000-year-old Daci Temple and a few minutes’ walk from Chunxi Road station. For the little ones: high chairs in the restaurants, and the pedestrianised lanes of Taikoo Li right outside the door.

Day 1 · Friday, 18 December

Arrival in Chengdu

Chengdu

Today marks the start of your adventure. Take the day to settle into Chengdu, and if you arrive early, explore Taikoo Li, the vibrant shopping district the hotel sits in.

Airport meet and greet. Your local guide will meet you in the arrival hall with your private vehicle on standby to take you to your hotel.

First stop: Chengdu. There is a popular saying: “Once you arrive in Chengdu, you never want to leave.” Spend even a few days here and it is easy to understand why. In Sichuan’s laid-back capital, life unfolds over cups of jasmine tea, lively mahjong games, bubbling hot pot, and shaded park paths. Yet Chengdu is more than slow living. It is a dynamic city of students, artists, entrepreneurs, and food lovers, where old teahouses sit alongside creative neighbourhoods and contemporary restaurants. Home to the beloved giant panda and some of China’s boldest flavours, Chengdu offers a warm, vivid introduction to Sichuan’s culture, creativity, and famously unhurried way of life.

Day 2 · Saturday, 19 December

Panda Base, Kung Fu Tea and the Kuanzhai Alleys

Chengdu

In the morning, get up close to the pandas that call Chengdu home. In the afternoon, immerse yourself in the city’s culture with a kung fu tea performance and a stroll down the Kuanzhai Alleys.

Chengdu Panda Base. Despite making their way into zoos around the world as diplomatic gifts from China in the 1970s, the best place to see everybody’s favourite black and white bears is in their hometown, Chengdu. In the Chengdu Panda Breeding Center, you will learn about the diet, reproduction, and conservation of these adorable creatures, and have plenty of opportunities to watch them do what they do best: eat, sleep, and roll around.

Kung Fu tea performance. China’s traditions of tea and kung fu are almost as old as the country itself. It is the marriage of the two, in the form of kung fu tea, that is much newer and wholly unique to Chengdu. Gaze in awe as the performer carries out a series of dazzling movements inspired by tai chi and kung fu, all culminating in the tea landing artfully in the cup. Then, awaken your inner tea master as you try your hand at gracefully pouring your own cup before tasting the fruits of your labour. After the performance, wander through the park for the ultimate people-watching experience, watching the locals as they dance their cares away or enjoy a roaring game of mahjong.

Kuanzhai Alleys. The Kuanzhai Alleys are a dynamic collision of traditional architecture and modern culture, where Qing Dynasty buildings play host to some of Chengdu’s best coffee shops, bars, galleries and boutiques. Rub shoulders with locals who love to roam these quirky lanes on their day off and tuck into some of the city’s traditional snacks.

Day 3 · Sunday, 20 December

Qingyang, Wuhou and Wenshu Temples

Chengdu

Today, visit three of the region’s most historically significant temples, then spend the rest of your Sunday unwinding at the hotel or exploring at your own pace.

Qingyang Temple. Qingyang Taoist Temple is the birthplace of Lao Zi, the founding father of Taoism, which promotes the principle of wuwei, “do nothing.” Hidden throughout the grounds, under the open sky, nestled amidst the leaves, are small idols to different Taoist deities. Discovering them is discovering both the temple and Taoist thought.

Wuhou Temple. Wuhou Temple is the resting place of Zhuge Liang, one of the most famous military strategists in China. The Martial Marquis of Shu during the Three Kingdoms period (220–280 AD), Zhuge Liang’s wit and tact have rendered him immortal in military tales, and statues around the temple celebrate his life and accomplishments. After your visit, make your way to the local teahouses and shops lining the nearby Jinli street.

Wenshu Monastery. Wenshu Monastery is one of China’s largest and most well-preserved buildings of its kind. Its sloping roofs, upwardly flicked at the corners, towering colonnades, and incense-perfumed halls date back over 1,000 years. Your exploration of this working historical holy site allows you to experience the tranquil atmosphere and observe the daily rituals of monks and nuns, offering a glimpse into the spiritual life of the monastery.

Red Panda Forest Park and on to Qingcheng Mountain

Chengdu → Qingcheng Mountain

Meet the red pandas in the forests of Dujiangyan, then continue into the mountains for two nights at Six Senses.

Drive to Qingcheng Mountain. The ride from Chengdu to Qingcheng Mountain will take around 2 hours.

Red Panda Forest Park. Nestled in the lush surroundings of Dujiangyan, the Red Panda Forest Park is a specialised conservation and education centre dedicated to the protection and breeding of red pandas. Rather than a traditional zoo, the park offers visitors the chance to observe these charming animals in naturalistic forest settings and learn about their behaviour, conservation, and daily lives. It is a particularly engaging experience for families and wildlife enthusiasts, with opportunities for closer encounters under the guidance of the park’s professional staff.

Day 5 · Tuesday, 22 December

Qingcheng Mountain*

Qingcheng Mountain

Qingcheng Mountain is one of the birthplaces of Taoism. Known as “the most peaceful and secluded under heaven”, it is where Zhang Daoling founded the Way of the Celestial Masters in the 2nd century AD, and it is now a UNESCO World Heritage Site together with the nearby Dujiangyan irrigation system.

Explore. Ride the cable car up the mountain, the views opening up as it falls away, then walk along the trail to explore the temples on the mountain.

Day 7 · Thursday, 24 December

The Terracotta Warriors

Xi’an · Christmas Eve

Christmas Eve begins with one of the world’s great archaeological finds, followed by a quiet afternoon at the hotel before Christmas Eve dinner.

The Terracotta Warriors. In 1974, while digging a well in the vast countryside outside of Xi’an, a group of farmers made a legendary discovery. They struck a chamber containing 8,000 soldiers belonging to China’s fearsome first emperor, a terracotta army buried beneath the earth to guard his tomb for eternity. Less than an hour outside the city, you’ll spend two hours coming face-to-face with row upon row of these ancient guardians, an experience no trip to China is complete without.

Remainder of the day at leisure. Spend the afternoon at the hotel, and end the day with a Christmas dinner on Christmas Eve.

Day 8 · Friday, 25 December

City Wall, Dumpling Making and the Wild Goose Pagodas

Xi’an · Christmas Day

Spend Christmas Day walking the ancient City Wall, making dumplings for lunch, and visiting the Wild Goose Pagodas.

Traverse Xi’an’s ancient City Wall. Ascending to an imperious height of 12 metres, the only way in or out of downtown Xi’an is to pass through cavernous openings in the oldest, largest, and best-preserved city wall in China. Without a doubt, the best way to experience the fortifications is by walking on top of them. Keep your head on a swivel while making the circuit, taking full advantage of the commanding views of the ancient city within and the modern metropolis outside.

Art you can eat: dumpling making. Xi’an claims to be the birthplace of Chinese dumplings, and whether or not that’s true, local chefs have elevated the humble staple to the most sophisticated culinary heights. Head to Xi’an’s most popular dumpling restaurant, where these little hand-pinched dough parcels of ground meat and vegetables are crafted into all shapes and sizes, from walnuts to roses, and have a go yourself at making everybody’s favourite Chinese snack.

Wild Goose Pagodas. Standing proudly beyond the city walls are the handsome Wild Goose Pagodas, imaginatively named Big and Small for reasons which don’t need explaining. Reportedly modelled on the pagodas of India as seen by an 8th-century itinerant monk, these two structures are not only among the oldest in Xi’an but important symbols of a city whose architecture is heavily influenced by styles from as far afield as Beijing and Iran.

Day 10 · Sunday, 27 December

The Forbidden City and the Hutongs

Beijing

Two sides of Beijing: the imperial splendour of the Forbidden City, and the hutong lanes where everyday local life carries on.

The Forbidden City. Step inside the Forbidden City, a massive imperial palace once home to China’s emperors and the geographic centre of this endless metropolis. The gates were shut to all but the royal household and their entourage of eunuchs and concubines for 600 years until, in 1924, a powerful warlord gave the last emperor just three hours to leave.

Into the hutongs. Ask the residents of Beijing’s hutong alleyways, the crisscrossing mazes of courtyards and alleyways which make up the heart of the city, and they’ll tell you that anyone who lives further out isn’t really a Beijinger. You’ll see elders whiling away the night with a game of Chinese chess or mahjong, shopkeepers selling traditional snacks like jianbing (pancakes) and baozi (steamed buns), and hear calls announcing the arrival of the knife sharpener or cardboard collector. It is in these ancient neighbourhoods where the capital’s distinct culture was generated, and where it continues to thrive today.

Day 11 · Monday, 28 December

The Great Wall at Mutianyu

Beijing

A day trip to one of the best-preserved sections of the Great Wall, with a gondola up and the option of a toboggan ride down.

Rising from the sea in the east before extending 5,000 miles to the deserts of China’s far west, this is arguably the greatest man-made wonder on earth: the Great Wall. Head to the stunning Ming Dynasty ruins outside Beijing, where lonely guards far from home once kept a weary watch over the lands beyond.

One of the best-preserved sections of the Ming Dynasty Great Wall can be found at Mutianyu, a stretch of immense brickwork snaking through rugged terrain two hours north of Beijing. With gondola access up and the option of either a gondola or toboggan ride down, you can decide how long you wish to hike along this stunning section of the wall.

Day 12 · Tuesday, 29 December

The Temple of Heaven and the Summer Palace

Beijing

A final day of sightseeing, from the Temple of Heaven in the morning to the gardens and lake of the Summer Palace.

The Temple of Heaven. Once the place where the Ming and Qing emperors came to pray for good harvests, the Temple of Heaven is crowned by the triple-roofed Hall of Prayer for Good Harvests, set within a vast, tree-lined park. Explore the grounds, discovering the temple’s architecture, history, and its most secluded people-watching spots, where locals gather in the mornings for tai chi, dancing and games of cards.

The Summer Palace. Live an afternoon of royal splendour just 30 minutes from Beijing at the Summer Palace, whose regal pathways and pavilions were built by the Empress Dowager Cixi, a former concubine who went on to become the most powerful woman in China. Unwind with a leisurely stroll around the garden’s centrepiece, Kunming Lake.

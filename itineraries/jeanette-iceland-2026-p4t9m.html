Two Proposals

One country. Two very different ways in.

October is when Iceland tilts toward darkness and the aurora begins in earnest. Both itineraries place you here for 11 nights at the start of prime Northern Lights season — with starting slightly earlier in October as an option because October 8 is a new moon, which may make for a more intense Northern Lights experience amidst darkened skies. While still having longer daylight hours to take in all that Iceland has to offer.

We've put both options side by side so you can see exactly what each one looks like, day by day.

The Options

Choose your journey

Option A

The Guided Classic

Nordic Visitor Small Group Tour + Independent Extensions

11 nights 8 – 19 Oct Group up to 16

Option A starts with a 7-day Northern Lights-centric small group tour with a well-reviewed and established tour operator, Nordic Visitor, which covers most of the southern coast. You then return to Reykjavik for 2 nights, and on one of the days you will take a semi-private (max 4 pax) Superjeep tour to the beautiful Thorsmork — or the valley of Thor — a stunning nature reserve in the Icelandic highlands named after Thor. Your final two nights in Iceland invites you to explore a hidden corner of Iceland, Husafell, a wealth of natural beauty with numerous hiking trails and the chance to get up close to major waterfalls and visit a canyon bath.

Snæfellsnes Peninsula Kirkjufell Mountain Glacier Lagoon Diamond Beach Vatnajökull Ice Cave Þórsmörk Day Trip
~SGD 8,000 per person ~SGD 16,000 for 2
Option B

The Immersive Retreat

Midgard Base Camp + Private Extensions + Hotel Husafell

11 nights 7 – 18 Oct Private & small group

Option B starts with a 4-day Northern Lights Adventure run by a boutique, family-run tour operator, Midgard Adventure. You'll return to their accommodation Midgard Base Camp throughout the 4 days, and get hosted at an Icelandic home. After that, enjoy a private daytrip with Midgard and experience a side of Iceland that is not in the guidebooks — you'll explore hidden canyons and hike where tourists don't go, before spending a night in the Aurora Igloo South. The next day, head over to Husafell, with an optional Snaefellsnes daytrip enroute touring the western side of the south coast. Arrive in Husafell, where you will spend a leisurely 3 nights in the rural quiet of the area. Finally, return to Reykjavik, where you get a chance to explore the city for 2 nights, before staying the night in an airport hotel for an early morning flight home.

Northern Lights nightly Super Jeep Þórsmörk Private Hidden Iceland Tour Katla Ice Cave Glacier Ice Walk 3 Nights at Husafell

Where Both Journeys Meet

Shared destinations

Four places appear in both itineraries — experienced at different moments, in different ways, at a different pace.

Reykjavík  ·  ION City Hotel

Iceland's capital rewards time: geothermal pools, the food on Laugavegur, the harbour, the museum circuit. ION City Hotel is a Design Hotels property in the business district. Both itineraries begin and end here — the city bookends both journeys.

Option A  ·  Nights 1, 7–8 Option B  ·  Nights 1, 9–10

The Golden Circle

Þingvellir's rift valley, where two tectonic plates pull apart. Gullfoss dropping in two tiers into the canyon. Strokkur erupting every few minutes. Iceland's most famous loop — in October, the crowds have thinned and the light has a particular quality that the summer never quite delivers.

Option A  ·  Day 3, guided Option B  ·  Day 2, from Midgard

Hotel Husafell  ·  West Iceland

A quiet property surrounded by lava fields and glacier views, with Hraunfossar — a long stretch of springs emerging directly from the lava — five minutes away. Both itineraries include time here; Option B gives you three nights instead of two, and the slower rhythm that comes with them.

Option A  ·  2 nights Option B  ·  3 nights

Northern Lights

October marks the start of serious aurora season — long nights, stable temperatures, and clear skies when the weather cooperates. Both itineraries place you in dark-sky locations throughout. The difference: Option A leaves viewing to weather and opportunity, while Option B builds nightly Northern Lights watching into the programme, from Midgard's rooftop hot tub.

Option A  ·  Opportunity-based Option B  ·  Structured nightly at Midgard

Helping You Decide

Which journey fits?

Option A

Consider the Guided Classic if...

  • Iceland's iconic sights — Snæfellsnes, the Glacier Lagoon, Diamond Beach — feel non-negotiable for a first trip
  • A well-supported small-group tour with all logistics handled suits your travel style
  • You want a trusted, established operator managing the full first week
  • Covering more of the country's geography matters more than staying longer in fewer places
  • You're comfortable with a group of up to 16 for the main tour

Option B

Consider the Immersive Retreat if...

  • The Northern Lights are the priority — Midgard has structured nightly viewing built into the programme from the first evening
  • This trip feels closer to a retreat: present, unhurried, time to absorb each place rather than move through it
  • A private "not in any guidebook" day — just the two of you — is the kind of experience you're drawn to
  • Depth over breadth: fewer destinations, more time in each one, no alarm for the next location
  • Missing some of the iconic sights is a trade-off you're willing to make for something more personal

Based on what you've shared about this trip — pace, presence, and the Northern Lights as the priority — Option B is the closer fit. That said, both work, and we can talk through either in as much detail as you'd like before deciding.

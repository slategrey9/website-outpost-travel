Why a human still books your holiday

Notes from the world's biggest luxury travel show, on judgement, light switches and artificial intelligence.

By Kristin Lim · 4 min read

I spent last week at the world's biggest luxury travel show. Fifty meetings, four days, a lot of coffee. I went expecting to hear about new hotels and new islands, and I did. What I did not expect was to hear the same quiet argument made twice, from two different stages, by people who had never met.

Both said the same thing. In a world filling up with artificial intelligence, the value of a human has gone up.

That sounds like the sort of thing a room full of travel advisors wants to hear about itself. So I paid close attention to the reasoning, because comforting ideas are usually the ones worth checking.

More choice, harder decisions

The way people plan has stretched at both ends. Some now book eighteen months out, because they have realised time is the scarcest thing they have and they want something worth looking forward to. Others book inside a week, on impulse, because they can. What has grown in the middle is not certainty. It is noise. There has never been more information about where to go, and it has never been harder to choose. When everything is available and everything looks the same, a moodboard is easy and a decision is hard.

Artificial intelligence is very good at the moodboard. It will give you a hundred beautiful options in a second. What it cannot give you is the phone call that gets you the table, the room, the guide who is actually good, the afternoon that was never listed anywhere. It cannot tell you that the photo is doing a lot of work, or that the quiet season is the better season, or that the second hotel is worth twice the first for reasons the website will never mention. That part still runs on relationships, and relationships still run on people.

A brand is not what you say about it. It is what people feel and remember and repeat when you are not in the room.

One of the speakers put it more simply than I can. Then she turned it on the advisors: your recommendation carries more weight than any campaign. When someone knows you, and trusts you, they book what you suggest. Your judgement travels with you.

I have felt the truth of this from the other side. The trips my clients remember are almost never the ones with the longest list of inclusions. They are the ones with a moment in them. A door that opened. A dinner that should not have been possible. A morning that was planned so lightly it felt like luck. None of that is a product you can add to a cart. It is judgement, applied on your behalf, by someone who has been there.

The light switch

The other speaker, who thinks about the psychology of experience for a living, said something that has stayed with me. When he books a holiday, he is not looking at the palm trees. He knows what the sea looks like. He is looking, without quite realising it, at the quality of the light switch. The small thing that tells him whether the rest of the place was cared for. That is the work I love most, and it is the work a search cannot do. Reading the small things, so you never have to.

So no, I do not think the machines are coming for this. If anything they are clearing the ground. They will happily handle the noise, the comparing, the first hundred options. What is left is the part that was always the point. Someone who knows you, knows the places, and quietly makes the two fit.

That is what we do. You tell us where you are dreaming of going. We take it from there.

Kristin
